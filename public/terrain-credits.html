A real mountain. Your own climb.

Our Ben Nevis scene uses geographic elevation data, with the Mountain Path, water and woodland geometry from OpenStreetMap. East, north and height use the same units; the mountain is not stretched vertically. The colours, lighting and surface artwork are illustrated.

Terrain

Elevation: Mapzen / Tilezen Terrain Tiles, resampled to a 50 metre grid. The mesh is an approximation of the terrain, not a survey of every rock or tree. The summit target is 1,345 Laundry Metres; the source elevation grid is retained without altering its summit samples.

Europe terrain data produced using Copernicus data and information funded by the European Union — EU-DEM layers. Global GMTED2010 and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey. United Kingdom terrain data © Environment Agency copyright and/or database right 2015. All rights reserved. See the providers’ attribution and licence information.

Other expedition previews

Fuji, Matterhorn, Kilimanjaro, Denali and Everest use separate elevation grids. Their snow and vegetation palettes are illustrative seasonal treatments; mapped glacier boundaries are not represented. These are visual previews of locked future expeditions. Data: Fuji, Matterhorn, Kilimanjaro, Denali, Everest.

ArcticDEM DEMs were created from DigitalGlobe imagery and funded under National Science Foundation awards 1043681, 1559691 and 1542736. 3DEP data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey.

Path and surroundings

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database Licence (ODbL). Mountain Path: relation 4004229. Data obtained 6 October 2026.

Download the scene’s geographic data. The OpenStreetMap-derived route and feature database remains available under ODbL. Elevation values retain their respective providers’ terms.

Woodland cover is based on mapped polygons; individual tree species and placement are illustrative. Terrain outside the mapped feature area still uses elevation data, but its land cover is approximate. Seasonal snow, vegetation and weather are not live observations.

Laundry Metres are game progress. This scene is not a navigation map.

Illustrated game artwork

The basket companion and foreground trail are original generated illustrations based on the owner's selected Laundry Mountain art direction. The character uses separate body and limb assets so accepted progress controls its walk and checkpoint celebration. The washing is attached to the body.

Climb view combines this illustrated foreground with the elevation-rendered Ben Nevis backdrop. Its close-up stone path and sock stops are game presentation, not a surveyed recreation of each section of the Mountain Path. Full mountain shows your progress on the mapped route. No full-scene background image replaces the geographic renderer.
